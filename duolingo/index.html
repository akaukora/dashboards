Position in the French course over time, reconstructed from cumulative XP — not recorded data

Duolingo records only where you are now, with no history of when you passed each unit — and the report emails never mention level at all. So the line is reconstructed: your units spread across the weeks in proportion to the XP you earned, anchored to end on your recorded position.

It does survive one real test. Duolingo's own Score for the course is 91 of 129, and mapping unit 499 through the published CEFR score bands (A1 0–29, A2 30–59, B1 60–99, B2 100–129) predicts 89.6 — within 1.4 points. That check is why the shape is trustworthy even though the model is simple. Its weak point is timing rather than position: early units are shorter than later ones, so the late-2024 dates likely run ahead of reality. Read any single date as ±a few weeks, and note the curve ends on the position from the data export (unit 494, Score ≈89) rather than the slightly newer duome figure above, so its final point or two is a touch conservative.

Time and lessons come from Duolingo's weekly progress-report emails (hello@duolingo.com) — a Proton Mail export plus reports recovered from an old Gmail account. Duolingo sends one report per course, so a week often arrives several times over with very different figures; the one kept is whichever carries the account's long French streak, since a secondary course's report covers only a sliver of the week and sometimes logs more minutes than French did. Faded bars mark weeks no report covers, which is a gap in the mail rather than a week without studying. One week (Oct 19–25, 2024) used a template that stated no XP at all, so only its XP is unknown.

Hatched slate bars are estimates, shown in their own color so they never pass for measured data. They cover the weeks with no report email: . Their XP comes from Duolingo's own league scores (leaderboards.csv in the account data export); since the league week ends about three days off from the report week, treat these as close approximations rather than exact figures. Their time is derived from that XP using the median XP-per-minute of the twelve nearest reported weeks — local rather than lifetime, because the rate climbs from about 7.9 in early 2025 to 13.8 by mid-2026, and a lifetime average would overstate the recent gaps by roughly a third. Lessons are left blank, as nothing in the league data implies them. Every estimate is replaced by real figures automatically once a report email for that week turns up.
